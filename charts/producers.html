s3log: producers × writers × lag

s3log-bench against s3-mem (in-memory S3) with the lean client, on one 2-core VM. Closed-loop writers: each submits a command, waits for its result, repeats. Producers: 1 to 5, or every writer also runs a producer (and there are no others). 10 s per run, one run per point, capped at 4 minutes (DNF = did not finish). Every finished run passed all protocol checks.

What it shows

  • With a 200 ms lag, one producer is fastest: 10,405 commands/s at 4,000 writers. Each extra producer fetches every command again: command GETs per command grow with the producer count. At 4,000 writers, 2 producers give 9,023 commands/s, and 3–5 give 6,700–7,400.
  • Below 1,000 writers the producer count doesn't matter. Each writer waits about the lag for its result, so throughput is roughly writers ÷ wait, and the machine has CPU to spare.
  • With a 1,000 ms lag, the wait sets throughput for every fixed producer count: about one command per writer every 1.4 s.
  • Every writer as a producer collapses: it keeps up at 100 writers (372 commands/s, 14 command GETs per command), but at 250 writers it does 199 commands/s with 173 GETs per command, at 500 writers 60 commands/s with an 8.6 s median wait, and from 1,000 writers on runs don't finish within 4 minutes. The work grows with writers × producers: every producer polls the effect log and fetches every command, and they all race for each effect.
  • Everything runs on one 2-core VM: the bench process (writers, producers, reader) and s3-mem compete for CPU, so extra producers cost throughput here directly.
