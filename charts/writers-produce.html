s3log: writers that produce, instead of fixed producers

Can writers take turns producing, so that no dedicated producer process is needed? Rows are strategies; the first two are the extremes from the producer matrix.

What it shows

  • No fixed producers: after its PUT, a writer waits; if no new effect appeared meanwhile, it runs a producer cycle itself. With a fixed random wait, many writers' waits expire during one producer's cycle, so they all produce: up to 500 of 500 writers, fetching each command up to 340 times. Throughput collapses from 500 writers on.
  • Adaptive: the wait is 2–3× the average gap between effects the writer has seen, within the bounds shown, and a writer also stays out if the watermark moved (producers raise it every cycle). Up to 1,000 writers only 3–13 writers produce and each command is fetched 1.0–1.5 times; 871 commands/s at 1,000 writers.
  • But the wait sets its own pace: a writer produces only after a stall, runs one cycle and waits again, so effects arrive about once per wait (0.5–1 s) and every command waits about that long. One fixed producer runs cycles back to back: 3,482 commands/s at 1,000 writers.
  • From 2,000 writers on it collapses anyway: to be able to produce, every writer reads every effect (up to 16 effect GETs per command), the bench process saturates, cycles slow down, and waits expire mid-cycle again (170 writers produced at 4,000).
  • Same setup as the producer matrix: s3log-bench against s3-mem with the lean client, one 2-core VM, closed-loop writers, lag 200 ms, 10 s per run, capped at 4 minutes. Every finished run passed all protocol checks.
